## What <Years /> years on sliding doors teaches you

Most door problems aren't mysteries once you've seen enough of them. The three Joseph sees most are worn-out rollers, dirty or damaged tracks, and locks that have worn out or slipped out of line. They're often connected, which is why our [sliding glass door repair guide](/) starts from what the door is doing rather than which part to buy.

The coast is harder on doors than anywhere inland. Salt speeds up corrosion on every metal part, and humidity and sand wear the rest, so a waterfront home in Miami or Fort Lauderdale needs more maintenance than a house further west.

Old doors are their own puzzle. Older PGT and CGI models and some discontinued aluminum brands can take real effort to find parts for, and the hard part is usually identifying the exact model. More on that on our [patio door repair](/patio-door-repair) page.

And the most expensive mistakes tend to happen before anyone calls: forcing a heavy door, adjusting rollers without knowing how the door is put together, or ordering a roller because it looks right. Our [roller replacement](/sliding-door-roller-replacement) page explains how to measure one properly.

## An honest repair-or-replace call

Joseph won't recommend a repair just to get a job. When the frame is badly damaged, the glass is compromised, or corrosion has spread through several parts at once, a new door is the better long-term value, and that's what you'll hear. When it's worn rollers and a dirty track, you'll hear that instead, even when you called expecting to buy a door. The [replacement page](/sliding-glass-door-replacement) lays out where that line sits.
