## Two kinds of condo slider in one city

For a condo owner, sliding glass door repair in Deerfield Beach depends a lot on where the building is. Some condos are close to the ocean, with a slider that opens onto a balcony and takes salt air every day. Others are inland in Century Village East, where separate condominium buildings, each with its own name, sit inside one large community off Hillsboro Boulevard.

The parts that fail are the same in both: rollers, tracks and locks, the three problems our founder sees most in Florida homes. What changes is how fast they wear, and who has to sign off before anything about the door changes.

## When sliding glass door repair in Deerfield Beach means a lighter door

A slider that takes your whole body weight to open isn't something you have to live with. The panel sits on two small wheels in its bottom rail. When the sliding door rollers wear flat or their bearings seize, the wheels skid instead of turning, and the door gets harder to move every month. If that sounds like your door, the cause is almost always at the bottom of the panel.

The usual fix is new rollers plus a cleaned track. We check the track first, because new wheels on a pitted rail wear out early. Then we match the rollers by measurement, not looks, and set the panel just high enough to clear the sill without letting air and water past the seals.

The lock tends to come along for the ride. When a roller wears, that end of the panel drops and the latch lands below its keeper, so raising the panel often fixes the latch too. A snapped handle or a worn latch hook gets replaced on its own. Neither one means a new door.

Inland doesn't mean trouble-free, though. A track that hasn't been vacuumed in years can make a door just as stubborn as salt can.

## What we look for on a balcony slider

The first thing we do is slide the door with one hand and listen. A grinding sound means a seized bearing. A scraping sound means the wheels have worn flat and the bottom of the panel is riding on the rail. A door that moves fine but rattles has usually dropped low on one side.

Then we look at the sill. On a beachfront balcony, the weep holes (small drain slots at the outer edge of the sill) clog with sand and salt crust, and a sill that can't drain sends rain onto the floor inside. We clear those before touching the rollers, since a repair doesn't help much if water sits in the track.

Once the panel is out, the roller housings tell the story. Orange streaks along the track and rust on the housing mean salt got into the steel. That's when we suggest stainless rollers, if they're made in the right size for your door. Inland, at Century Village East, the same inspection usually turns up a packed track and cracked plastic wheels instead.

## When a condo door has to be replaced

A new door changes things. Broward County is in the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone, so a replacement needs a product approval that covers HVHZ use, filed on the county's shared window and door form. Under Florida condo law, the board can't refuse hurricane protection that meets its adopted specifications, but it can hold you to the building's existing look. Ask the city's planning staff whether the Community Appearance Board needs to see the change too.

## Screens get opened more than the glass

On a ground-floor unit or a balcony, the screen is often the panel that moves most, because it stays shut while the breeze comes through. It rides on its own small rollers, and when they wear, the screen drags or jumps off the track. A screen that won't stay on its rail is usually the smallest fix of the visit.

Screen rollers are quicker work than anything on the glass panel, so it makes sense to do both in one visit. Our [screen door page](/sliding-screen-door-repair) covers screen rollers, mesh and spline.

## Before you call about a Deerfield Beach door

Check your declaration of condominium first. Some declarations make the association responsible for exterior windows and doors, and then the repair may be the association's to arrange. If the job is yours, a photo of both bottom corners of the moving panel and the full length of the track tells us most of what we need.

A label etched into a corner of the glass helps us look up the right roller. Older aluminum doors from brands that no longer exist can take some digging, and we'll tell you if that's the case.

If the door won't budge right now, don't force it. Send the photos first, and we'll tell you if it sounds like a simple repair or something bigger. The same goes for a hinged lanai door.
