## Our target

We aim to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 at Level AA, and we're working toward WCAG 2.2 Level AA, the current version of the standard.

## What we've done

- Every page has a single main heading and a logical heading order, with landmarks for the header, navigation, main content, sidebar and footer.
- Text and background colors were chosen and checked to meet WCAG AA contrast ratios.
- The site works with a keyboard alone. A "skip to content" link appears on the first Tab press, and focus is always visible.
- Menus, the symptom finder and the FAQ use native HTML disclosure elements, so they work with assistive technology even if JavaScript fails to load.
- Diagrams are built with real text, carry text descriptions for screen readers, and every diagram has a caption that states its main point.
- Animation is minimal and stops entirely when your device is set to reduce motion.
- Form fields have visible labels, and error messages are announced to screen readers.
- Text can be enlarged to 200% without loss of content.

## Known limitations

- Photos from real jobs will be added over time. Each will have a text description, but some older uploads may have shorter descriptions than we'd like.
- Links to outside documents, such as manufacturer care guides and government PDFs, go to content we don't control, and some of those files aren't fully accessible.

## Tell us about a problem

If something on this site doesn't work for you, please let us know and tell us which page and what happened. We'll get you the information another way in the meantime, by phone or email.
