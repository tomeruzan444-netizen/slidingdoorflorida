## 165 miles of waterways

Fort Lauderdale has 165 miles of inland waterways inside its limits, by the [city's own count](https://www.fortlauderdale.gov/Residents). Add the Intracoastal and the beach, and a lot of sliders here live with salt air every day. That shapes sliding glass door repair in Fort Lauderdale more than anything else, because salt goes after the metal parts first.

Our founder puts it plainly: salt speeds up corrosion on metal components, and homes near the ocean need maintenance more often than homes inland. On a canal-front door, that means the parts you can't see wear first.

## What sliding glass door repair in Fort Lauderdale usually finds

The wheels under the panel sit lowest, in the track where salt water collects after rain and stays damp for days. A steel roller inside an aluminum panel, riding on an aluminum track, is a pairing that corrodes faster with salt water around it. Our page on [sliding door roller replacement](/sliding-door-roller-replacement) explains the chemistry and compares the wheel types.

The short version is a trade-off. Stainless rollers cost more and hold up better near salt water. Nylon won't rust, but heat and UV break it down, and a heavy panel can flatten it. On a door that faces the water, we lean toward stainless when the right size exists for that door.

If you typed sliding glass door repair near me because the door has started to grind, the rollers are the first suspect.

Corroded sliding door rollers grind, then skid instead of rolling, and the panel gets harder to move each month. Orange streaks along the track are another sign. Sliding glass door roller replacement fixes the drag, but only if the track under it is still smooth.

Salt reaches the lock too. If you searched patio door repair near me because the door won't lock, the door itself may be fine.

A latch that's stiff or won't catch may only need cleaning and an adjustment. When the lock body is corroded inside, a patio door lock replacement is the fix, and a sliding door handle replacement often goes with it since both mount through the same holes.

## Condo doors and the permit question

More than a third of Fort Lauderdale homes, 36.3%, are in buildings with 20 or more units, according to the Census Bureau's [2019-2023 American Community Survey](https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDP5Y2023.DP04?g=160XX00US1224000). For those owners, a sliding glass door replacement involves both the association and the city.

Florida's new permit exemption for small residential jobs took effect July 1, 2026, but it won't cover most of those doors. The city's [HB 803 guide](https://www.fortlauderdale.gov/Government/Departments/Development-Services/HB-803) lists condominium, multifamily and apartment properties as not exempt, along with window replacements and properties in a flood hazard area. It doesn't mention door replacement either way, so ask the city before you assume anything.

Broward is in the Florida Building Code's High-Velocity Hurricane Zone, so a new door also needs a product approval that covers that zone. A sliding patio door repair, by contrast, keeps the door you already have.

The screen panel gets the same salt on even smaller rollers, so sliding screen door repair is worth doing at the same visit. Whatever the sliding door repair, we check the track before the rollers, and you hear what we found before any work starts.

## Inland is a different door

A few miles west, the same slider lives an easier life. In [Davie](/sliding-glass-door-repair-davie), away from the coast, doors wear from grit and daily use more than salt. A good nylon roller is usually fine there. Within sight of salt water, it often isn't.
