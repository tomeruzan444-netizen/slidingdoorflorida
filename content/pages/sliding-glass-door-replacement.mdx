## What Florida requires when a door is replaced

Replacing an exterior sliding glass door in Florida is permitted work, with a product that has to be approved for where you live. Three things drive it.

Start with the permit. The Florida Building Code requires a permit for work like this, and exterior door replacement isn't on the code's list of exempt work. Counties handle it in their own ways. Some run a simpler "size for size" replacement permit, but they still want the new door's product approval and an inspection. Your local building department is the final word on what it needs.

Next is product approval. Exterior doors sold for Florida homes need state product approval, which shows up as an FL number, or approval through a local program. The state's product approval database lets you check any door, filtering for approval inside or outside the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone, for impact resistance, and by design pressure (the wind load, positive and negative, the door is rated to handle).

Then there's where you live, and since our crews work across Florida, we deal with both sides of this line. The Florida Building Code defines the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone as Broward and Miami-Dade counties, from Miami and Hialeah up to Deerfield Beach. Doors installed there have to be approved for HVHZ use, and Miami-Dade issues its own Notice of Acceptance (NOA) for products that pass its testing.

Everywhere else, including Palm Beach County just to the north, a door is checked against the statewide product approval for its wind zone instead. That isn't a free pass. Much of coastal Florida outside the HVHZ still falls in a wind-borne debris region, where glass openings need impact-resistant glazing or an approved impact covering such as shutters. In [West Palm Beach](/sliding-glass-door-repair-west-palm-beach), the city's historic districts add a design review on top of the permit.

The code also has narrow exceptions for older homes that replace only a small share of their glass in a year. Whether one applies to your house is your permit office's call, not ours or a salesperson's. Our [Plantation page](/sliding-glass-door-repair-plantation) shows how that 25% rule plays out on an older Broward house.

One timing note: the 9th edition of the Florida Building Code takes effect on December 31, 2026. Wind-related provisions were among those reviewed in that cycle, including how wind-borne debris regions are defined. A door approved and permitted under the previous edition is fine. A new project should be checked against the current one.

## Impact glass or shutters

Florida lets you protect a glass opening in either of two ways: impact-rated glass in the door, or an approved covering you put up before a storm. Both satisfy the code where protection is required.

Impact doors use laminated glass, which federal safety rules describe as layers of glass bonded to plastic interlayers. When it cracks, the pieces stay stuck to the interlayer and the opening stays closed. That's the appeal: no panels to store, nothing to put up when a storm turns toward you, and protection on the nights you aren't home. The trade-off is weight and cost. Impact panels are heavy, which is why [rollers rated for the load](/sliding-door-roller-replacement) matter so much on them later.

Shutters cost less up front and work on a door you already have, as long as the existing door is in good shape. They only protect you when they're up.

Insurance is worth a conversation with your agent before you buy. Florida law requires insurers to offer windstorm mitigation discounts that include opening protection, and a uniform mitigation verification form is used to document it. We don't give insurance advice, but we can tell you what product approval and impact rating a door carries.

## Who is allowed to replace your door

Since August 2024, Florida has had a state-certified window and door installation specialty contractor license. Its scope is installing, replacing and repairing exterior windows, doors and shutters without structural changes. General, building and residential contractors can do the work too, and a glass and glazing specialty license covers sliding glass doors.

Whoever you hire, check their license with the state licensing board. It takes a minute and shows the license type, status and any discipline.

## Frame, glass and configuration choices

Aluminum and vinyl are the two common frame materials for Florida sliders. Aluminum is strong for its weight, which allows slim frames around big glass panels, and it's common on impact doors. It also conducts heat, so better aluminum frames include a thermal break. Vinyl insulates better, but large vinyl panels need the right reinforcement to carry heavy impact glass. Both have to meet the same product approval for your location, so start there and compare within what qualifies.

For glass, read the energy rating label. It lists the U-factor (how much heat passes through) and the Solar Heat Gain Coefficient, or SHGC (how much of the sun's heat gets in). In Florida, the SHGC usually matters more, because keeping afternoon sun out is most of the cooling battle. Low-E coatings are the thin metallic layers that bring that number down.

One thing changed recently: the federal energy-efficient home improvement credit doesn't apply to doors installed after December 31, 2025. Don't count on it when you price a door now.

## From order to install day

Replacing a door is mostly paperwork and waiting, followed by one busy day. The order of things looks like this:

1. Measure the opening and note what's around it: stucco, tile, the threshold height, and whether it's a condo with association rules.
2. Choose a door with the right product approval and design pressure for the house.
3. Apply for the permit with the product approval documents.
4. Order the door. Impact doors are made to size, so this is usually the longest wait.
5. Install, then the building inspection.

On install day the old panels come out, then the old frame. The opening is checked for rot, level and square, then the new frame goes in on the fasteners and anchoring the product approval specifies, gets sealed against water, and the panels go in and get adjusted. The installation instructions in the approval aren't optional. The inspector checks against them. Some cities spell out exactly what they want to see, and our [Coral Springs page](/sliding-glass-door-repair-coral-springs) walks through one city's checklist.

If you live in a condo, and a lot of Miami and Fort Lauderdale homeowners do, check with your association before you sign anything. Many have rules on frame color, glass tint and who may work in the building.
