## Try the adjustment screw before you buy anything

A dragging door doesn't always mean a dead roller. Most patio sliders let you raise or lower each roller with a screw, and a panel that has simply settled low will drag exactly like one with a worn wheel. Five minutes with a screwdriver tells you which one you have.

Look at the two bottom corners of the moving panel. The adjustment holes sit there, either on the inside face or on the vertical edge, and they're often hidden under small plastic caps you can pry out.

1. Open the door about a foot so you can reach both holes.
2. Lift the panel slightly with one hand to take its weight off the roller.
3. Turn the screw a quarter turn by hand. Don't use a drill: it strips the screw and can crack the housing.
4. Close the door and check the gap between panel and frame. It should be even from top to bottom.
5. Repeat on whichever side needs it, a quarter turn at a time.

Don't assume clockwise raises the door. In its patio door guide, JELD-WEN documents rollers that go up when turned clockwise and others that go down, depending on how the screw is mounted. Pella's service sheet for its 250 Series simply says to raise or lower until the edge of the panel is plumb with the side of the frame. Make a small turn and watch what happens.

Going too high causes its own trouble. The same JELD-WEN guide says a panel set too high can leak air and water, so the target is just enough lift to clear the track and keep the weatherstrip tucked out of sight.

## Signs the roller itself is finished

If adjusting doesn't fix the slide, the wheel is the problem. Any of these means replacement:

- The screw turns but the panel doesn't move, which usually means the housing is broken inside
- A wheel that is cracked, chipped, or worn flat on one side so it thumps as it rolls
- A wheel that won't spin by hand once the panel is out
- Rust on the axle or housing, or orange streaks along the track
- The door still grinds after the track has been vacuumed clean

Grinding can come from the rail too. If the track has dents or a worn ridge, new rollers will ride badly and wear out early, so it's worth reading about [track repair](/sliding-door-track-repair) before you order parts.

Most of these come with ball bearings in the hub, and the bearing is often what fails first. JELD-WEN puts it bluntly in its aluminum door care guide: metal hardware "is not totally corrosion proof," and plastic hardware "can deteriorate from ultraviolet light, heat, cold, and chemical exposure." Every option has a weak spot. Inland, a good nylon or steel roller is usually fine. Within sight of the water, we lean toward stainless when the right size exists for your door.

## Why salt air is so rough on rollers

It's chemistry, and it gets worse wherever two metals touch. When two different metals sit together with salt water between them, one corrodes in place of the other. NOAA's [explanation of galvanic corrosion](https://www.omao.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/documents/ClassicGalvanicCorrosion.pdf) shows aluminum as the metal that gives way.

A sliding door puts a steel roller inside an aluminum panel, riding on an aluminum track, in air full of salt. Add the damp that sits in a sill track for days after rain, and you have the setup that pits housings and locks up bearings. JELD-WEN's care guide recommends hardware maintenance at least twice a year, and monthly in coastal areas.

## Heavy impact doors need rollers rated for the weight

Impact-rated panels use laminated glass and heavier frames, and the rollers carry all of it. FGIA's roller standard, AAMA 906, tests roller assemblies at the weight the manufacturer rates them for, cycling them under load 10,000 times. Ratings under the 2021 version span from 75 pounds to more than 600 pounds per assembly.

A light-duty roller under a heavy panel is carrying more than it was ever tested for. Match the rating as well as the size, and don't take a listing's word for a rating its maker doesn't publish.

## Doing it yourself vs. calling us

If the adjustment fixed it, you're done. If you need new rollers and you're handy, the job is possible, with one serious caveat: the glass panel has to come out of the frame on most doors. JELD-WEN's care guide says removing patio door panels "can be dangerous and should be performed by service personnel or at least with additional help."

Some aluminum doors let you change rollers without pulling the panel, by taking off the side pieces of the frame. Others don't. Either way, set the new rollers to their lowest position before installing, adjust both sides equally afterward, and never hit aluminum with a metal hammer face, since it dents easily.

Call us if the panel is heavy or impact-rated, if the screws are rusted in place, or if the correct roller for your door isn't made anymore. Old and discontinued doors are where a parts drawer and some experience matter.

## What a roller swap looks like on-site

We check the track first, because new rollers on a bad rail is money wasted. Then the panel comes up and out, the old rollers come out of the bottom rail, and we measure them against what we carry. The new ones go in at their lowest setting, the panel goes back on the track, and we adjust until the gaps are even and the latch lines up with its keeper.

That last step matters. Andersen's service notes point out that once a panel's height changes, the latch receiver may need adjusting too, which is why [a latch that stops catching](/sliding-door-lock-and-handle-repair) often gets sorted out during a roller job.

## Related roller problems

The screen panel outside your glass door rides on its own, much smaller rollers. When the screen drags or jumps out, it's a different part and a quicker fix, covered on our [screen door repair](/sliding-screen-door-repair) page.

Mirrored closet doors are the indoor cousin. Many of them roll on bottom rollers too, but the top of the door has to sit high enough in its channel to stay put, so [a closet door that keeps jumping out](/sliding-closet-door-repair) is usually a height problem before it's a wheel problem.

When the rollers are fine but the bottom rail of the panel itself is corroded or split, there's nothing solid left to hold a roller. That's one of the few roller-related problems where [a new door](/sliding-glass-door-replacement) is the honest answer. For anything else your door is doing, start with our guide to [sliding glass door repair](/).
