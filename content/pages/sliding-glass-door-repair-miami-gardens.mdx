## A city of houses, most of them owned

Two out of three homes in Miami Gardens are detached houses, and two out of three are lived in by the people who own them.

That makes sliding glass door repair in Miami Gardens a homeowner's decision most of the time, made by someone who plans to keep living with the door. If you've owned your house for decades, this page was written with you in mind.

The houses are older than people expect for a city that only incorporated in 2003. The median home was built in 1970, and almost a third went up in the 1950s alone. Most of the city was built out decades before it became one.

On a door that has slid back and forth for decades, the part we'd look at first in any sliding door repair is the one that keeps it shut.

## A latch only keeps the door shut

Locks are one of the three problems our founder sees most, next to rollers and tracks, and the founder's own description names both causes: the parts wear out, or they fall out of line.

A sliding door latch is a hook on the panel that swings into a keeper on the frame. It holds the door shut. On its own it does little to keep someone out, and no patio door repair changes that until a second layer goes on.

Two questions matter for sliding doors: are they secured with auxiliary locks or pins, and are there screws in the track so the panel can't be lifted out. Both point at the same weak spot. A worn slider with a tired latch and nothing else is the door those questions are about.

Our [lock and handle page](/sliding-door-lock-and-handle-repair) goes through the extra layers, from track blockers to pin locks, and how to fit anti-lift screws without making the door bind. A break-in scare is a good moment to add one of those layers while the latch gets fixed.

## Sliding glass door repair in Miami Gardens when the latch stops catching

Most of the time the lock isn't broken. The panel has dropped. The rollers wear flat over the years, the panel sinks a fraction of an inch, and the hook swings under the keeper instead of into it. On a slider that has carried its panel since the 1960s, that's a common story.

So sliding glass door repair in Miami Gardens often starts at the bottom of the panel, not the handle. New rollers or a simple height adjustment bring the hook back to the keeper, and then the keeper gets fine-tuned in its slotted holes.

A new lock only comes into it when the hook is bent or snapped, or the lever spins without moving anything. If the wheels are cracked rather than just low, the rollers come first, and the lock often needs nothing.

Handles fail on their own schedule. A cracked or loose handle gets replaced, matched by the distance between its screws and the thickness of the panel. The screen panel's latch and rollers wear out too, and they're a small job to add to the same visit.

If the back door won't lock, check one thing first: lift the handle end slightly and push the latch. If it catches, the door has sagged, and the lock itself is fine.

## If you replace, plan to be home for the inspection

Sometimes a door can't be saved. Replacing an exterior door in Miami Gardens always needs a permit and an approval for the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone. The part homeowners don't expect is the inspection: you or a representative have to be there with the installer, and every opening has to be left without interior trim or caulking so the inspector can see the shims and fasteners. The inside of the door gets finished after the inspector leaves, so if the room has to look done by a certain date, plan for the trim to come last.
