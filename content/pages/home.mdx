## Most sliding door failures start at the bottom

A sliding glass door panel rides on two small rollers set into its bottom rail. Those wheels carry the whole weight of the glass, and they roll along an aluminum rail that also has to drain rainwater. When either one wears, everything above it goes out of line.

<JosephSays>
  <p>The three most common problems I see are worn-out rollers, damaged or dirty tracks, and faulty sliding door locks. These are issues I come across regularly in Florida homes.</p>
</JosephSays>

On our jobs, those three are usually one problem in disguise. A roller wears flat or its bearing seizes, and the panel drops a fraction of an inch on that end. Now it drags, the gap along the top goes uneven, and the latch hook lands below the keeper so the door won't lock. That's why a door that "won't lock" often gets fixed with [new or readjusted rollers](/sliding-door-roller-replacement) and no lock at all.

Height matters in both directions. A panel set [too high lets air and water past the seals](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/jeldwencanada1-jeldwen-p523-e8d9/media/Project/JeldWen/JeldWenCanada/Articles/Documents-Video/Care-Maintenance/JPG012_5.pdf), and one set too low drags on the sill. The right setting is just high enough to clear the track.

## Why South Florida is hard on sliding doors

A door a few blocks from the beach in Fort Lauderdale lives a harder life than the same door in a dry climate, and it shows up in the parts.

<JosephSays>
  <p>Salt exposure can accelerate corrosion on metal components, while humidity and sand can contribute to premature wear. Homes near the ocean generally require more frequent maintenance than homes further inland.</p>
</JosephSays>

Near the water, corroded rollers and pitted tracks make up a big share of what our crews replace. Salt settles in the track and on the rollers and stays damp there for days after rain. Loewen, a door manufacturer, tells owners in coastal areas to [clean and inspect every 3 months instead of every 6](https://www.loewen.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Maintenance-of-Loewen-Products-1.pdf), which matches what salt does to a door left alone.

Sand rides in on every trip from the pool or the beach. Mixed with old grease, it turns into a paste that grinds roller wheels down and scores the track. Once the rail is pitted, new rollers wear out fast, so [track repair](/sliding-door-track-repair) sometimes has to come first. And the plastic parts (many rollers, guides and handle pieces) break down in heat and UV. A west-facing slider on a lanai bakes all afternoon.

Then there are storms. A 2024 research report for the [Florida Building Commission](https://www.floridabuilding.org/fbc/publications/Research_2024-2025/Final%20Report%20-%20Reviewing%20the%20Standards%20for%20Wind-Driven%20Rain%20Through%20Tracks%20of%20Sliding%20Glass%20Doors%20systems.pdf) quotes FEMA's Hurricane Ian team, who found wind-driven rain got into homes "primarily through SGDs and soffits," including houses built in 2021. Hurricane season runs June 1 through November 30, with the peak in August, September and October, according to [NOAA](https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/outlooks/hurricane2026/May/hurricane.shtml). Spring is the time to clear the sill and test the lock. The week a storm gets a name is not.

Where you live in Florida changes the rules for a new door. Broward and Miami-Dade sit in the Florida Building Code's High-Velocity Hurricane Zone, which holds a replacement door to the strictest product approval rules in the state. Our [door replacement](/sliding-glass-door-replacement) page explains what that means if your door is past saving.

## Repair first, replace when the frame is finished

Rollers, tracks, locks, handles and weatherstrip are parts. They wear out and get swapped, and the door keeps going. We'd rather replace a roller than sell you a new door when the frame is solid.

One of our jobs shows what that looks like. The homeowner was sure the door had to go: it barely moved, and they assumed the frame was damaged. It wasn't. Worn rollers and years of debris in the track were the whole problem, and the [full story is on our roller page](/sliding-door-roller-replacement).

A new door does make sense sometimes, and we'll tell you when it does. Our founder draws the line here:

<JosephSays>
  <p>If the door frame is severely damaged, the glass is compromised, or the system has extensive corrosion and multiple failing components, replacement may be the better long-term investment.</p>
</JosephSays>

Glass is its own case. Fog between the panes means the seal around the insulated glass unit has failed and the drying agent inside is saturated, so no cleaning reaches it. The glass unit has to be replaced, and whether that's possible without a new door depends on how the panel was built.

A cracked pane is a safety issue. Federal rules list patio-type sliding glass doors among the openings that need [safety glazing under 16 CFR 1201](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-II/subchapter-B/part-1201), and the same rule notes tempered glass can't be cut or ground after tempering without breaking. A replacement pane is made to size. It's never trimmed down from a bigger sheet.

## Two checks to do before you call

Neither needs tools beyond a vacuum and a screwdriver.

1. **Clean the track and the drain slots.** Vacuum the rail end to end, then brush out the small weep holes on the outside face of the sill. Some water in the sill during rain is normal. It's supposed to collect there and drain out through those holes.
2. **Look at the gap.** Close the door and compare the gap between the panel and the frame at the top and the bottom. If it's wider at one end, the panel is out of level. The adjustment screws sit at each lower corner of the moving panel, sometimes behind small plastic plugs. Lift the panel slightly to take weight off the roller, and turn a quarter turn at a time by hand, not with a drill.

Which direction raises the door depends on the roller design, so watch the gap after each turn. Stop if the screw spins without moving anything or you can see a cracked wheel. That roller needs replacing, and pulling a full glass panel out of the frame is a two-person job.

On lubricant, follow your door maker's care sheet. Manufacturers disagree (some specify silicone, others say avoid it), but none recommend heavy grease in a track that collects sand.

## What happens when we come out

We start by watching the door move. Slide it open and shut, listen for where the grind starts, and check the gap all the way around. Then we lift the panel off the sill to look at the rollers and the track underneath, because that's where most of the answers are.
