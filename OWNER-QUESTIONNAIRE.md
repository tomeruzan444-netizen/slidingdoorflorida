# Owner questionnaire

Your answers are what make this site sound like you instead of like every other door company. Short, plain answers are perfect. Voice notes are fine too. Nothing below gets published until you've answered it; until then the site shows placeholders.

Fill in what you can, skip what you can't, and send photos as separate files.

---

## Part 1: Business facts (these go into `content/business.ts`)

1. Business name, exactly as it appears on your Google Business Profile.
2. Website domain (or the one you plan to buy).
3. Main phone number. Do you accept texts with photos at that number?
4. Email address for quote requests.
5. Do customers visit you at an address (storefront), or do you only go to them (service-area business)? If storefront, the full address.
6. Florida license type and number, exactly as shown on myfloridalicense.com.
7. Insurance: general liability and workers' comp carrier or wording you're comfortable publishing.
8. Year you started doing this work.
9. Founder name, and the names of anyone who goes to customers' homes (first names are fine).
10. Hours, day by day.
11. Counties and cities you cover, matching your Google Business Profile.
12. Google Business Profile link, and links to Yelp, BBB, Facebook, Nextdoor or any other listings.
13. Who will review the site's technical content? (Name and role. This becomes the "Last reviewed by" line. Leave blank if no one.)

## Part 2: Scope (what you do and don't do)

14. Which do you work on: sliding glass doors, screen doors, closet doors, hinged French doors, multi-slide / stacking / pocketing doors? Anything on that list you turn down?
15. Do you replace glass only (a fogged or cracked insulated glass unit), or only whole panels/doors? Do you handle impact (laminated) glass?
16. Do you do full door replacements? If yes: do you pull the permits, which brands do you install, and are you comfortable with HVHZ (Miami-Dade/Broward) jobs?
17. Do you replace track sections or whole sills? Do you install stainless track caps?
18. Do you add security hardware (anti-lift screws, pin locks, security bars, keyed conversions) or pool door alarms?
19. Are you OK publishing price ranges? If yes, give ranges with conditions (for example, "roller replacement on a standard two-panel door: $X to $Y, more for impact panels"). If no, we'll explain what drives price instead.

## Part 3: Your experience (this is what makes the content authentic)

20. On coastal jobs, what do you see most? (Rusted rollers, pitted tracks, clogged weep holes, corroded screws, sun-cooked weatherstrip?) What's different on inland jobs?
21. Which door brands do you run into most in Florida homes? Any that are especially hard to find parts for?
22. What's the most common mistake people make trying to fix a sliding door themselves?
23. When do you tell a customer a door isn't worth repairing? Give a real example.
24. What does a first visit look like? Do you charge a diagnostic or trip fee? How long are you usually on site for a roller job, a track cap, a lock?
25. Which parts do you carry on the truck? (Common rollers, caps, locks, handles, screen mesh?)
26. What warranty do you give on parts and labor?
27. Do you rescreen on site or in a shop? Which mesh types do you stock (standard, pool & patio, no-see-um, pet, solar)?
28. Closet doors: what usually breaks, and do you replace mirrors yourself?
29. Condos and HOAs: anything specific you deal with (building rules, work hours, insurance certificates)?
30. What's your typical response time for a call? Only give a number you can keep.

## Part 4: Job stories and photos

For each service below, 3 to 5 real jobs. For each job: town or area, what the customer said was wrong, what you found, what you did, and before/after photos if you have them. Real photos only (phone photos are great). No stock images, no AI images.

- Roller replacement
- Track repair (cleaning, caps, sill work)
- Lock and handle repair
- Screen door repair
- Patio door repair (French or multi-slide, if you do them)
- Closet door repair
- Door replacement

Also helpful: photos of you and your team at work, your truck, and close-ups of worn parts you've pulled out (a flat roller, a pitted track, a clogged weep hole). Those make the best illustrations on the site.
