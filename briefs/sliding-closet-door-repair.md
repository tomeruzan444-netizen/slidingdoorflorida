## 1. Search intent

Who is searching and what situation they are in:

- **Main situation: a closet door that has stopped working right now.** The door jumped off the top track, dropped at one end, scrapes the floor, rubs the other panel, or will not close flush to the wall. The visitor wants to know if it is a quick fix or a part replacement, and whether they can do it or need someone.
- **Mirrored door owners in particular.** Mirror bypass doors are heavy and the visitor is often nervous about lifting a large glass panel. They want to know how the door comes off, whether the mirror is safety-backed, and what to do with a cracked or desilvering mirror.
- **"Which door do I have?" confusion.** Many people cannot name their system (bypass, bifold, pocket, top-hung vs bottom-rolling), so they cannot search for the right part. The infographic answers this.
- **Secondary: repair vs replace.** Older wood bypass doors on worn hardware, or mirror doors with black edges near the coast. Visitor wants to know when new rollers/track are enough.
- **Commercial intent is mixed:** many searchers are DIY (Lowe's, This Old House, iFixit rank). The page should serve the DIYer honestly and make clear what situations are worth a call (heavy mirror panels, bent tracks, broken frame corners, damaged mirror).

Not this page's intent: patio sliding glass doors, screen doors, exterior tracks (those have their own pages).

## 3. Unique angle (one sentence)

Before any part is replaced, figure out whether your closet door hangs from the top or rolls on the bottom, because that one fact explains why it jumped the track and what fixes it, with extra care for heavy safety-backed mirror panels in Florida's salt air.

## 6. Infographic

**Type:** side-by-side visual comparison (three columns or three cards on mobile) plus a short "how to tell" decision path. HTML/SVG with real text, `figure` + `figcaption`. Must differ from other pages' infographics (patio pages use tables/cross-sections).

**Question it answers:** "What kind of closet door do I have, and is it top-hung or bottom-rolling?"

**Exact content:**

Column A: **Bypass (sliding) doors**
- Simple line drawing: two panels overlapping in the opening, front and rear track.
- How it moves: panels slide past each other; only half the closet is open at once.
- Two versions (sub-labels):
  - *Top-hung:* weight on wheels/hangers in the header track; small guide on the floor between the doors keeps them in line. Common on wood or panel doors (Johnson 2200 example).
  - *Bottom-rolling:* weight on rollers in a floor track; plastic or wheel guides at the top keep the door upright. Common on mirror doors (Renin, Contractors Wardrobe, Slimfold examples).
- Typical trouble: comes off top track, drops at one end, rubs the other panel, gap at the wall.
- Where to look: adjustment screw on the hanger (top-hung) or on the bottom roller near the wall edge (bottom-rolling).

Column B: **Bifold doors**
- Drawing: two or four hinged panels that fold toward the side jamb.
- How it moves: panels fold in pairs; a pivot at top and bottom of the panel next to the jamb, a guide or roller in a top track on the other end.
- Typical trouble: door drags or binds when folding, pivot pops out of the bracket, panels don't meet in the middle.
- Where to look: bottom pivot bracket and top pivot (location of height adjustment varies by brand).

Column C: **Pocket doors**
- Drawing: one panel sliding into the wall cavity.
- How it moves: hangs from wheels in a track inside the header; small guides at the floor at the wall opening.
- Typical trouble: door drops or scrapes, comes off the track inside the wall, won't fully retract.
- Where to look: hangers adjust for height; door removal needs the split header/stop trim removed (Johnson 1500 example).

**"How to tell" path (below the columns, 3-4 steps):**
1. Does the door fold? Yes = bifold. No, go to 2.
2. Does the door disappear into the wall? Yes = pocket. No = bypass, go to 3.
3. Slide one door aside and look at the floor. A track with the door's wheels sitting in it = bottom-rolling. Only a small guide block or nothing = top-hung.
4. Confirm at the top: wheels/hangers visible behind the header trim = top-hung; only plastic guides or small guide wheels = bottom-rolling.

**Figcaption (takeaway, to be written in page voice):** the door type and where its weight sits tell you where to adjust and why it left the track.

Accessibility: each column a list in reading order; icons decorative (`aria-hidden`); decision path as an ordered list, not only arrows.
