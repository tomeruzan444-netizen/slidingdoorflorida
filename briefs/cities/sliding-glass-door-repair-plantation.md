## 1. Search intent

A Plantation homeowner with a slider that drags, won't lock or has come off its track, often in an older single-family house. Some are weighing repair against replacement on a door that has been in place for decades and want to know what a new door involves in the city (permit, approvals, HOA).

## 3. Unique angle

Plantation was planned so neighboring homes don't look alike, so doors vary house to house: parts are matched by measurement, the frame decides repair vs. replace, and the house's age can decide what a replacement must meet.

Original hypothesis (1950s-70s neighborhoods, median year built) was only partly verifiable: the 1953 incorporation is verified, but no primary source for median year built was reachable (census.gov and api.census.gov blocked or require a key). ## 5. Business experience used

- We match parts by measurement, not looks (site practice; Joseph answer 6).
- Older PGT, CGI and discontinued aluminum doors can be hard to source parts for; identifying the exact model is the challenge (Joseph answer 3).
- Rollers, tracks and locks are the three most common problems (Joseph answer 1).
- Repair vs. replace criteria: damaged frame, compromised glass, extensive corrosion with multiple failing components (Joseph answer 5).
- We check the track before the rollers (site practice).
No job stories, prices, times or neighborhoods used.
