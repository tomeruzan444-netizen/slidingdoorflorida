## 1. Search intent

The searcher has a door that rides badly or a sill that holds water, and they think the track (the raised rail and channels in the bottom sill) is the cause. Situations:

1. **Door bumps, grinds or drags** and they can see sand, dents, grooves or a flattened rail. They want to know if cleaning is enough, whether a snap-on track cap works, or whether the track/sill must be replaced.
2. **Water sits in the track or spills onto the floor** after rain. They may not know weep holes exist or that they clog.
3. **Maintenance seekers** asking how to clean the track and which lubricant to use (silicone spray, grease, WD-40), often getting conflicting advice.

Some are DIYers shopping for a repair cap (the SERP has Prime-Line track caps at Home Depot, Lowe's, Ace, Tractor Supply). Give them the fit and install facts, then the cases where the problem is the sill, frame or rollers.

The page should move from cheapest to most involved: clean, clear drainage, straighten, cap, replace a track section, and when the sill/frame itself is the problem (link to replacement page, do not cover it).

## 4. Facts and sources (check date for all: 2026-11-26)

Legend: **Verified** = fetched and read. **Snippet** = seen only in search-result text; re-check before publishing. **UNVERIFIED** = no adequate source.

### Track cap (repair track)

1. **Prime-Line stainless patio door repair track (cap) install sheet D 1579-INS:** sizes D-1583 (6 ft), D-1579 (8 ft), D-1580 (10 ft), stainless steel; cross-section drawing shows **1/4 in. (6 mm)** and **3/16 in. (5 mm)**. Steps: "Remove the old cover and clean the track. Apply a thin bead of RTV-108 silicone adhesive. Place the track cap into position and put a 14" to 16" block of wood at least 1" thick on top, begin tapping the wood with a hammer to force the track into position. Keep moving down about 1" every couple of hits." Note on sheet: drawings are typical and may not show an identical part. Verified (Prime-Line PDF hosted on Home Depot image server, © 2011 Prime-Line Products).
   https://images.homedepot-static.com/catalog/pdfImages/61/61222b82-1a6d-4f46-93ce-22a69a17bbbc.pdf
2. **The cap fits over the existing rail; it needs the original rail in place.** Snippet (Home Depot Q&A answer). Consistent with item 1's "snap over" install, but re-check wording before quoting.
3. **Some buyers find their rail is wider than the cap opening.** Snippet (Home Depot review). Page use: measure rail width and height before buying; no claim about how common it is.
4. **Cap is effectively permanent once bonded.** Snippet (Home Depot review: "once installed with epoxy, it's not coming back off"). Manufacturer specifies RTV silicone, not epoxy; use only as "plan on it staying".

### Replacement track sections

5. **Aluminum replacement track sections are sold, cut to length and screwed or riveted in place; old rail removed by scoring and bending.** Snippet (All About Doors and DK Hardware listings). UNVERIFIED as a general method for Florida impact doors; owner must confirm whether they replace track sections and on which door types.

### Bent or worn rail

6. **Check straightness along the full length; avoid excessive force when straightening, which can weaken the metal; call a pro when the frame is damaged or out of square.** Snippet (This Old House; page not fetched). Re-check before citing.
7. **Filing down track dings restored operation in one forum case.** Snippet (Fine Homebuilding forum). Anecdotal; use only if owner confirms they do this.

### Cleaning and lubrication (manufacturers disagree)

8. **JELD-WEN (Aluminum Windows and Patio Doors, JCM001, 12/17):** suggested lubricant table lists "Spray Silicone: Use for slider tracks, jamb liners and weatherstrip. Do not spray directly onto hardware surfaces. Spray onto clean cloth to apply." For tracks: vacuum or wipe, apply spray silicone to a dry soft cloth and wipe onto the track; "Do not apply silicone to pile (fuzzy) weatherstrip." Caution: "Silicone may cause some hard plastic parts to become brittle." "Always thoroughly clean hardware before lubricating." Lists white/lithium grease as a multipurpose lubricant for moving parts (not specifically for tracks). Verified.
   https://pdf.lowes.com/productdocuments/19107816-1cdb-4f22-abd2-e68d75a86980/01120390.pdf (JELD-WEN document hosted on Lowe's PDF server)
9. **JELD-WEN JCM001 precautions:** do not use vinegar-based cleaners, citrus cleaners, paint removers, window cleaners, brick/siding washes or abrasive cleaners on hardware finishes. Clean twice a year, "monthly in coastal areas". Verified.
10. **Loewen:** "We recommend using waterproof marine lubricant and marine grade grease for long term performance. Avoid the use of silicone based sprays or lubricants. Silicone can cause some plastic parts to become brittle." "Use compressed air to remove sand and salt from tracks." Verified. Loewen, "Maintenance of Loewen Products".
    https://www.loewen.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Maintenance-of-Loewen-Products-1.pdf
    Page use: present both makers side by side; the advice is "use what your door's maker specifies; never use a product that holds grit". The homepage brief has a lubricant FAQ (its Q15) flagged "may belong to track page"; this page should own it.
11. **Milgard prefers silicone-based over oil-based lubricants because oil attracts dirt; coastal hardware lubrication at least twice a year; remove coastal sand immediately.** Snippet only (milgard.com returned 403). UNVERIFIED for direct citation.
12. **PGT care guide track/lubricant guidance.** UNVERIFIED (pgtwindows.com returned 403; no track lubricant text seen even in snippets).
13. **WD-40 (regular) and grease on tracks hold dirt.** Snippet only (newspaper column, Glass Doctor, forum). Not a manufacturer source. Use the manufacturer framing in items 8 and 10 instead.

### Weep holes, sill drainage, water

14. **JELD-WEN weep system (JCM001), Verified:** "damage could develop in the structure around the unit if water does not drain properly from the sill area. Weep systems are designed to drain water to the exterior." Steps: regularly clean the sill track with a vacuum or damp cloth; pour a small amount of water (1 cup) into the interior sill track: if it drains through the exterior weep holes, the system is clear; if not, insert a thin wire into the exterior weep hole, "Do not insert the wire farther than necessary to clear the blockage because the insulating glass unit may become damaged"; repeat until water runs clear; if not successful, call a service technician.
15. **U.S. DOE Building America Solution Center (developed by PNNL), "Clean Window Weep Holes" (updated 2025-07-30), Verified:** weep holes drain water out of the frame; if clogged, trapped water can accumulate within the frame. Test by pouring water into the track; clear with a pipe cleaner or small drain brush, compressed air can help; gently dislodge material without pushing it deeper; caulk is a known blockage: remove with a putty knife, utility knife or flathead screwdriver; solvent options listed; acetone may damage surfaces.
    https://basc.pnnl.gov/diy-guides/clean-window-weep-holes
16. **Florida Building Commission research report (FIU/UF), "Reviewing the Standards for Wind-Driven Rain Intrusion through Tracks of Sliding Glass Doors Systems during Hurricanes", revised Nov 8, 2024, prepared under Section 32 of HB 1021 (2024).** Verified. Quotes FEMA Hurricane Ian MAT: water intrusion "primarily through SGDs and soffits", including 2021-built homes; "Some SGDs were found to lack adequate sill risers"; "The entire State of Florida is within a hurricane-prone region." A TAC member "stressed the importance of keeping weep holes open to allow a product's water management system to allow any water that may enter to exit through weep holes."
    https://www.floridabuilding.org/fbc/publications/Research_2024-2025/Final%20Report%20-%20Reviewing%20the%20Standards%20for%20Wind-Driven%20Rain%20Through%20Tracks%20of%20Sliding%20Glass%20Doors%20systems.pdf
    Overlap note: homepage and patio-door briefs also cite this report. On this page, use it only for the track/weep/sill-riser mechanics, not the general "Florida is hurricane-prone" framing.
17. **Water normally collects in the sill and drains out of weep holes; keep the area free of dirt, insects, leaves; clean with a small soft brush.** Verified, Loewen (URL item 10). Homepage owns the "is water in the track normal" FAQ; do not repeat that question here.
18. **Forensic engineer (Warren Forensics, 2025-06-11), Verified as a secondary expert source:** photo caption "Without the slot, the sliding door tracks would collect water and overflow"; water that builds up "spills over the edge of the inside"; clog causes include dirt, insects (hornets nesting in uncovered slots in southern areas), substrates or misinstallation covering holes, pressure washing faster than weeps can drain; "I have seen and heard of homeowners who seal weep holes with caulk in an attempt to stop water intrusion!"
    https://www.warrenforensics.com/2025/06/11/water-removal-in-windows-and-doors-part-1-weep-holes/
19. **Don't drill new weep holes; clean the existing ones.** Snippet (Bunnings forum). Not authoritative; owner to confirm their practice before use.
20. **Screen track can cover weep holes on some aluminum doors.** Snippet (Bunnings forum). Owner to confirm if seen in Florida.

### Aluminum, salt, corrosion

21. **Bare aluminum oxidizes; JELD-WEN says pay attention to "bare aluminum (edges and weep holes)"; oxidation wipes off as a dark, metallic residue; remove gently with a fine scratch pad or steel wool without scratching finished surfaces; car wax over enamel or anodized finish for protection.** Verified, JCM001 "Cleaning Aluminum Surfaces".
    Caution for page: steel wool residue on aluminum in salt air is a possible galvanic concern; this is not stated by JELD-WEN. Do not add our own warning without a source; owner input.
22. **Galvanic corrosion: aluminum in contact with a more noble metal in seawater/electrolyte becomes the anode and corrodes.** Verified, NOAA OMAO "Classic Galvanic Corrosion".
    https://www.omao.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/documents/ClassicGalvanicCorrosion.pdf
23. **Sea chloride is a main factor in atmospheric corrosivity; salt is an effective electrolyte; heavy salt deposits can attack aluminum's oxide.** Verified (secondary trade source), The Construction Specifier.
    https://www.constructionspecifier.com/selecting-aluminum-finishes-for-coastal-areas/2/
24. **Pitted or corroded track from salt specifically.** UNVERIFIED as a sourced track failure mode (mechanism in 22-23 is sourced). Owner experience needed. Note the roller page also cites NOAA/Construction Specifier; keep this page on the track/sill angle (pitting of rail, residue at weeps), not on rollers.

## 7. FAQ candidates (track/sill only; not shared with roller, lock, screen, closet, replacement or home pages)

Source note: WebSearch does not return the "People also ask" box. Sources are related searches, question-phrased result titles, retailer Q&A, and forum snippets (Bunnings, Fine Homebuilding, Hometalk, Green Building Advisor). No Reddit/Houzz threads surfaced.

1. **Can a sliding door track be repaired, or does it have to be replaced?** (SERP title pattern; Angi/This Old House) Note: many tracks can be cleaned, straightened or capped; a broken or missing rail needs a new section or sill.
2. **Do sliding door track repair caps actually work?** (Home Depot/Lowe's reviews and Q&A) Note: yes on an intact but worn rail; cap fits over the original rail with RTV silicone (Prime-Line); measure first.
3. **How do I know what size track cap I need?** (review complaints about fit) Note: measure rail height and width; Prime-Line D 1579 family drawing shows 1/4 in. x 3/16 in.
4. **Can I straighten a bent sliding door track?** (This Old House "crooked tracks") Note: small bends, gently, with a block; too much force weakens metal (Snippet). Owner input.
5. **How do I get sand out of a sliding door track?** (Florida phrasing; Loewen compressed air) Note: vacuum, compressed air (Loewen), damp wipe, dry. Avoid frequency (homepage owns "how often").
6. **What lubricant should I use on a sliding door track?** (forum, Angi) Note: follow your maker: JELD-WEN spray silicone on a cloth; Loewen waterproof marine lubricant and no silicone. Take from homepage Q15 at registry.
7. **Is WD-40 OK for a sliding door track?** (related searches) Note: no manufacturer source found recommending regular WD-40 for tracks; use the product your door's care sheet names. Keep factual, no brand bashing.
8. **Should I put grease on my sliding door track?** (related searches) Note: Loewen allows marine-grade grease; other sources say grease holds grit. Defer to maker.
9. **What are the little slots in the bottom of my sliding door track for?** (Angi/House Digest phrasing) Note: weep holes; they drain sill water to the outside (JELD-WEN, DOE).
10. **How do I unclog the weep holes on a sliding glass door?** (Milgard how-to title, DOE guide) Note: thin wire or pipe cleaner, compressed air; don't push debris deeper or insert wire too far (JELD-WEN, DOE).
11. **How can I tell if my weep holes are blocked?** (DOE, JELD-WEN test) Note: pour about a cup of water in the interior sill track; it should drain outside.
12. **Why does my sliding door track fill with water and spill onto the floor?** (Bunnings "track fills with water", GBA thread) Note: clogged weeps or water above the inner lip; storms push wind-driven rain into tracks (FBC report). Check against homepage Q6 ("water under my door when it rains"); keep this one track-specific or drop.
13. **Should I caulk my weep holes to stop leaks?** (Warren Forensics, forum) Note: no; it blocks drainage; remove caulk from weeps (DOE).
14. **Can I drill extra weep holes in my track?** (Bunnings forum) Note: forum advice is to clean existing holes instead; owner to confirm; drilling may affect product approval (UNVERIFIED, do not claim).
15. **What is the black residue on my aluminum sliding door track?** (forum phrasing) Note: oxidation of bare aluminum; JELD-WEN says it wipes off as dark residue, remove gently and wax finished surfaces.
16. **Why does my sliding door track have grooves worn into it?** (South Florida forum/blog phrasing) Note: grit and dragging wear the rail; cap if the rail is intact. Owner input on cause.
17. **Can I pressure wash my sliding door track?** (Warren Forensics) Note: pressure washing can push water in faster than weeps drain; use vacuum and gentle cleaning.
18. **Can the bottom track be replaced without replacing the whole door?** (SERP question) Note: often a cap or track section works; frame/sill damage needs assessment. Owner input; do not discuss full replacement.
19. **What is a sill riser on a sliding door?** (FBC report terminology) Note: the raised interior part of the sill that helps resist wind-driven rain; FEMA observed some Florida doors lacked adequate risers. Definition wording to be checked against a manufacturer glossary before publishing (UNVERIFIED as a formal definition).
