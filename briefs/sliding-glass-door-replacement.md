## 1. Search intent

The visitor is **planning, not in an emergency**. Typical situations:

- The door has had repeated repairs (rollers, track, locks) and they wonder whether to keep paying for fixes or replace it.
- Glass is fogged between panes (failed IGU seal), the frame is corroded (common on coastal aluminum), or the door leaks or lets in heat.
- They want impact protection so they don't have to put up shutters every storm, or their insurer or a wind-mitigation inspection mentioned opening protection.
- They're renovating, or their HOA or condo association has rules about door appearance.
- They've heard that "you need a permit" or "it has to be Miami-Dade approved" and want to know what applies to them.

What they need from the page: (1) a clear way to decide repair vs. replace, (2) what Florida requires for a replacement (permit, approved product, impact protection where required, licensed contractor), (3) the choices they'll face (impact vs. non-impact, aluminum vs. vinyl, configuration, glass), (4) the steps from quote to final inspection, and (5) how to check a contractor's license.

Secondary intent: cost. Without owner-approved prices, the page explains **what drives the price** (CONTENT-GUIDE §17).

## 7. FAQ candidates

Source notes: Reddit was not reachable with the research tool, and People Also Ask boxes are not visible through the search API. Questions below come from related searches, the SERP pages above (national cost guides, Florida county permit pages and FAQs, Houzz/GardenWeb threads) and common phrasing. Mark the source when adding to `content/faq-registry.json`, and check for duplicates there (the file doesn't exist yet). Keep 8 to 15 on the page.

Answer notes are facts to build from, not final copy.

1. **Do I need a permit to replace a sliding glass door in Florida?** (related searches; county pages) Yes, generally. FBC §105.1 requires a permit and replacement isn't on the exempt list; counties run specific window/door replacement permits (Orange, Miami-Dade, Lee). Details vary by jurisdiction. (4.2 items 5 to 10)
2. **Does a replacement sliding door have to be impact-rated in Florida?** (related searches; Charlotte County FAQ) In a wind-borne debris region, replaced glazed openings need impact glass or approved protection, unless the 25% exception applies (older homes, ≤25% of glazed area in 12 months). (items 19 to 23)
3. **What is the 25% rule for replacing windows and doors in Florida?** (county FAQs; Miami-Dade page) FBC-EB §707.4 exceptions. Explain with the 12-month window, the older-home condition and the pre-Sept 1, 1994 condition in the HVHZ. (item 21)
4. **Can I use hurricane shutters instead of an impact sliding door?** (Houzz threads; installer pages) Yes, the code allows impact-resistant glazing or an approved impact-resistant covering; shutters need their own product approval and permit (FBC §105.1 mentions impact-resistant coverings). (items 5, 19)
5. **What's the difference between a Florida Product Approval and a Miami-Dade NOA?** (related searches) FL# = statewide approval by the Florida Building Commission (553.842); NOA = Miami-Dade Product Control acceptance to HVHZ standards. FL approvals show whether they're approved for HVHZ use. (items 12 to 17)
6. **Do I need a Miami-Dade approved door if I don't live in Miami-Dade?** (related searches) Not by state law; outside the HVHZ a Florida approval for use outside HVHZ is the normal route. Your building department may ask for specific documents. (item 17)
7. **How do I look up the product approval for a sliding door?** (county "common errors" pages) floridabuilding.org product approval search (filter Exterior Doors, HVHZ, impact, DP); Miami-Dade Product Control search for NOAs; use the full number incl. the digit after the dot. (items 9, 14, 16)
8. **What license does a contractor need to replace a sliding glass door in Florida?** (related searches) Certified/registered general, building or residential contractor, or the state window and door installation specialty (61G4-15.100(1)(j)); glass and glazing specialty also covers sliding glass doors. Verify on DBPR. Local rules after July 1, 2025 are partly UNVERIFIED. (items 26 to 31, 35)
9. **How do I check if a door contractor is licensed in Florida?** DBPR Verify a Licensee (name/license number/city). (item 34)
10. **Can I replace my own sliding glass door in Florida?** (forums) Owner-builder exemption 489.103(7) lets owners pull the permit for their own home with onsite supervision; the product and inspection rules still apply. No legal advice. (item 32)
11. **What does design pressure (DP) mean on a sliding door?** (related searches) The positive/negative wind pressure rating; must meet what the site requires, which depends on wind speed, exposure and location on the house. (item 24)
12. **What do the U-factor and SHGC numbers on the label mean?** (energy searches) NFRC definitions; for Florida, SHGC (solar heat) matters most; FBC Energy sets SHGC 0.25 for glazed fenestration in zones 1 to 2 (recheck). (items 40, 45)
13. **Is low-E glass worth it on a sliding door in Florida?** (related searches) Spectrally selective low-E cuts solar heat gain without much loss of visible light (DOE). Keep it factual and avoid savings claims. (item 46)
14. **Aluminum or vinyl sliding door: which is better in Florida?** (Houzz/GardenWeb) DOE: aluminum strong but conducts heat (thermal breaks help); vinyl insulates better. Florida-specific claims need the owner/manufacturer (UNVERIFIED). (items 47, 48)
15. **What does XO or OXXO mean on a sliding door?** (related searches) X = sliding, O = fixed, read from outside. (items 49 to 51)
16. **How long does it take to get an impact sliding glass door?** (installer pages; forums) Lead time and permit time are owner data (UNVERIFIED). The answer should explain the steps: measure, order, permit, install, inspection.
17. **What affects the cost of replacing a sliding glass door?** (national cost guides) Price drivers only: size and panel count, impact vs. non-impact, frame material, glass package, HVHZ products, whether the opening changes (structural/stucco work), permit, disposal, condo/high-rise access. No ranges unless the owner approves.
18. **Do I need HOA or condo approval to replace a sliding door?** (SERP gap; county search found nothing) Often association documents require architectural approval; that is separate from the building permit. State it as a principle; no legal advice. (No primary source; keep it general.)
19. **Will an impact sliding door lower my insurance?** (related searches) Florida law requires insurers to offer actuarially reasonable windstorm mitigation discounts, including for opening protection, and to accept a uniform mitigation inspection form; actual effect depends on the policy and the whole house. Refer to the insurer. (items 56, 57)
20. **Is there still a tax credit for energy-efficient doors?** (related searches) The federal 25C credit doesn't apply to property placed in service after Dec 31, 2025 (IRS). No tax advice. (item 44)
21. **Should I replace before hurricane season?** (seasonal searches) Season runs June 1 to November 30 (NHC); because ordering and permits take time, planning ahead matters. Owner supplies real lead times. (items 53, 54)
