## 1. Search intent

- **Something broke:** the screen panel jumps off its track, drags, won't close, the mesh is torn (pets, kids, storms) or sun-rotted, or the frame is bent. The visitor wants to know: fix it, rescreen it, or get a new screen door.
- **Bugs are getting in:** Florida-specific. No-see-ums, mosquitoes and lizards get past a screen that "looks fine". Visitor wants to know if a finer mesh helps and what the trade-off is.
- **Upgrade decision:** pet-resistant screen, solar screen for a hot west-facing slider, or what holds up near the coast.
- Mixed DIY (buy screen/spline/rollers) and hire-out intent. Some searchers are condo/HOA residents or landlords.

**Gaps we fill:**
- A **sourced mesh comparison** (standard 18x16, 18x14 pool/patio, 20x20 no-see-um, BetterVue-type, pet screen, solar screen, aluminum) with real numbers where the manufacturer or distributor publishes them.
- The honest no-see-um caveat: 20x20 is sold for "most tiny insects", while extension guidance says standard screens let no-see-ums through and suggests even finer mesh; finer mesh also means less airflow.
- **Coastal/salt air:** Phifer's own FAQ recommendation for coastal and high-humidity climates (fiberglass, sun-control, TuffScreen, PetScreen), versus aluminum.
- **Spline sizing**, including the rule that thick screens (pet, solar) need one size smaller spline, from Phifer's chart.
- **Screen door rollers are a different part** from main-door rollers (spring-tension, 1"-1-1/4" wheels), and when a bent frame means replacement instead of rescreening.

## 3. Unique angle (one sentence)

In Florida the screen panel fails from sun, salt, sand, pets and tiny biting bugs, so the repair starts with picking the right mesh and spline for that door and checking the small spring rollers and frame, not just patching a hole.
